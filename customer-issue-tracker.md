# Customer Issue Tracker
## Sample Customer Support Portfolio Project

### Purpose
This tracker demonstrates how customer issues can be recorded, prioritized, assigned, monitored, and followed up until resolution.

*All entries below are fictional examples created for demonstration purposes.*

## Issue Tracking Log

| Ticket ID | Issue Summary | Priority | Status | Assigned Team | Next Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CS-001 | Customer cannot log in | High | In Progress | Technical Support | Review error details and follow up |
| CS-002 | Customer asks about subscription billing | Medium | Open | Billing Support | Verify account and billing information |
| CS-003 | Customer requests help using a feature | Low | Resolved | Customer Support | Confirm the guidance was helpful |
| CS-004 | Customer reports an unexpected error | High | Escalated | Product/Engineering | Share reproduction steps and monitor progress |

## Priority Guidelines

- **High:** The issue significantly affects access or an important service, or requires urgent attention.
- **Medium:** The issue needs timely investigation but has a reasonable workaround or limited impact.
- **Low:** A general question or request that does not significantly disrupt the customer's work.

Priority should be based on customer impact, urgency, and the company's support procedures.

## Status Definitions

- **Open:** The issue has been received and recorded.
- **In Progress:** Investigation or troubleshooting is underway.
- **Escalated:** The issue has been referred to a team with the required expertise or authority.
- **Resolved:** A solution has been provided and the outcome has been confirmed where possible.
- **Pending Customer:** Additional information is required from the customer.

## Ticket Handling Workflow

1. Receive the customer's request and acknowledge it.
2. Record the issue, relevant details, and customer impact.
3. Assign a priority based on urgency and impact.
4. Investigate within the scope of your access and authority.
5. Escalate unresolved or technical issues with clear supporting details.
6. Keep the customer informed and document follow-up actions.
7. Confirm the outcome and update the ticket status.

## Example of a Useful Ticket Note

**Ticket:** CS-001  
**Issue:** Customer cannot log in  
**Action taken:** Confirmed the reported error and suggested the approved password-reset process.  
**Current status:** In Progress  
**Next action:** If the issue persists, document the error details and escalate to Technical Support.  
**Follow-up:** Update the customer after receiving the next confirmed finding.

## Good Record-Keeping Practices

- Keep notes factual, clear, and professional.
- Record actions taken and the next follow-up step.
- Avoid recording passwords or unnecessary sensitive information.
- Do not mark an issue resolved until the required resolution criteria are met.
- Escalate promptly when an issue exceeds your authority.
- Protect customer information and follow company policies.
